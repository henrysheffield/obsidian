Tackling air conditioning for a classic 3-bedroom, 1-bathroom home in Oakleigh requires a solid game plan, especially when the house has no insulation. Before diving into the numbers, here is a candid reality check: cooling an uninsulated house in a Melbourne summer is like trying to keep a esky cold with the lid off. The heat from the roof cavity will radiate directly into your rooms, meaning any system you install will have to work significantly harder, consume more electricity, and require a higher kilowatt (kW) capacity than standard.

Here is a highly structured, visual breakdown of your options, costs, and local contacts to help you make an informed decision for the Drummond Street property.

### Air Conditioning Options & Effectiveness

For an uninsulated property, your choice of system drastically impacts both upfront costs and ongoing energy bills.

| **System Type**                  | **How It Works**                                                               | **Effectiveness in an Uninsulated House**                                                                                                                                      | **Best For**                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Split System (Reverse Cycle)** | Individual wall-mounted units in specific rooms (e.g., bedrooms, living area). | **Highly Effective.** You only cool the rooms you are actively using, minimising energy waste. This is the smartest choice for uninsulated homes.                              | Targeted cooling and heating; keeping upfront and running costs manageable.                       |
| **Ducted (Reverse Cycle)**       | Central unit pumps air through vents in the ceiling across the whole house.    | **Poor to Fair.** Ducts run through the blistering hot roof cavity. Without insulation, the system will bleed energy, and your power bills will skyrocket.                     | Whole-home comfort, but **strongly advised against** unless you install ceiling insulation first. |
| **Evaporative Cooling**          | Pulls hot outside air through wet pads to cool the house via ceiling vents.    | **Fair.** Cheaper to run, but it only cools (no heating) and struggles on humid Melbourne days. The lack of insulation means the house's thermal mass will still radiate heat. | Budget cooling, but requires keeping doors/windows open for airflow.                              |

### Estimated Costs (Melbourne Averages)

Because the house is uninsulated, you will likely need to size up the units (e.g., installing a 3.5 kW unit in a bedroom instead of a 2.5 kW unit) to handle the extra heat load.

| **System / Service**                 | **Unit Size (Capacity)**         | **Estimated Cost (Supply & Install)** |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Small Split System** (Bedrooms)    | 2.5 kW – 3.5 kW                  | $1,250 – $2,800 per unit              |
| **Large Split System** (Living Area) | 5.0 kW – 7.0 kW                  | $1,900 – $4,725 per unit              |
| **Multi-Head Split System**          | 1 Outdoor Unit, 3-4 Indoor Heads | $7,000 – $14,000                      |
| **Ducted Air Conditioning**          | Whole Home (Basic to Zoned)      | $8,400 – $18,000+                     |
| **Switchboard Upgrade**              | If your electrical board is old  | $200 – $800                           |

_Note: Premium brands like Daikin and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries will sit at the higher end of these price brackets but offer better efficiency and longer warranties._

### Installation Timeframes

The time it takes from the tradies pulling up in the driveway to handing over the remote depends heavily on the system chosen.

|**System Type**|**Estimated Installation Time**|**Notes**|
|---|---|---|
|**Single Split System**|4 to 8 hours|Usually completed in half a day. Back-to-back installations (indoor unit directly opposite the outdoor unit) are the fastest.|
|**Multi-Head Split System**|1 to 2 days|Running piping from multiple indoor heads to a single outdoor unit takes a bit more labour.|
|**Ducted System**|3 to 5 days|Highly invasive. Involves cutting holes in the ceiling, running extensive flexible ducting, and installing the heavy roof/outdoor units.|

_Keep in mind that during peak summer months (November to February), scheduling an installation can take several weeks due to high demand across Melbourne._
