## Arrhenius Acid
### Core Definition

An **Arrhenius acid** is any substance that dissolves and ionizes in water to release **hydrogen ions** ($H^+$), thereby increasing the overall concentration of $H^+$ ions in the solution.

### Key Characteristics

- **Solvent Limitation:** The Arrhenius definition is strictly limited to **aqueous (water-based) solutions**.
    
- **Neutralisation:** Reacts with an Arrhenius base (which releases hydroxide ions, $OH^-$) to form **water** ($H_2O$) and a **salt**.
    

### Reaction Mechanism

When an Arrhenius acid releases $H^+$ into water, the hydrogen ion immediately bonds with a water molecule to form a **hydronium ion** ($H_3O^+$):

$$H^+ + H_2O \rightarrow H_3O^+$$
