## Becquerel (Bq)


> [!ABSTRACT] Description
> Represents one nuclear decay or transformation per second. It is a measure of how many particles/photons are emitted by a radioactive source per second.
> 

### Key Ideas

- **1 Bq** = One nuclear decay per second
- Measures rate of decay **not** the physical amount of material
- Used to quantify radiation in food, water and environmental samples
    

---

### Examples / Problems

An average smoke detector can contain about 
$$37 ~kBq \quad \text{of Americium-241}$$
![[Becquerel (Bq)-1778227774082.webp]]
*This diagram shows how an americium source ionizes air particles and makes an ionization smoke detector work.*

- A smoke detector works by emitting [[Alpha Radiation]] to sense particles (like smoke) in the air
- Ionisation smoke detectors (use radioactivity as a tool) commonly used **Americium-241** which emits Alpha Radiation.
- Because alpha radiation is very heavy and cannot travel very far, and the americium sample is encased in a layer of foil and ceramic, it poses no radioactive risk.
- See more on how smoke detectors work: https://www.epa.gov/radtown/americium-ionization-smoke-detectors

### Flashcards

What does a Becquerel represent? :: A becquerel (symbol: **Bq**) represents ==the International System of Units (SI) derived unit of radioactivity==, defined as **one nuclear disintegration (or decay) per second**. It measures the rate at which an unstable radioactive material emits radiation, with higher values indicating greater radioactivity.
