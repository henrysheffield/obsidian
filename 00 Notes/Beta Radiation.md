> [!TIP] **Summary**
> Beta radiation is a form of ionizing radiation driven by the **Weak Nuclear Force**. It occurs when an unstable nucleus has an imbalance of protons ($p^+$) or neutrons ($n^0$).

### Key Characteristics

- **Description**: Emission of a beta particle—either an electron or a positron.
    
- **Mass**: Extremely small, approximately $\frac{1}{1800}$ to $\frac{1}{2000}$ the mass of a proton.
    
- **Ionizing Power**: Moderate.
    
- **Penetration**: Moderate; can be stopped by thin metal like aluminum foil.

### The Two Main Types of Decay

#### 1. Beta-minus ($\beta^-$) Decay

- **Process**: A neutron transforms into a proton, emitting a high-speed electron and an antineutrino.
    
- **Nucleus Change**: Atomic number ($Z$) increases by 1; Mass number ($A$) remains the same.
    
- **Example**: $^{14}_{6}\text{C} \rightarrow \text{ } ^{14}_{7}\text{N} + \text{ } ^{0}_{-1}\text{e} + \bar{\nu}$


> [!NOTE]
> The $\bar{\nu}$ symbol = antineutrino
> The $\nu$ symbol = neutrino
    

#### 2. Beta-plus ($\beta^+$) Decay

- **Process**: A proton transforms into a neutron, emitting a positron and a neutrino.
    
- **Nucleus Change**: Atomic number ($Z$) decreases by 1; Mass number ($A$) remains the same.
    
- **Example**: $^{A}_{Z}\text{X} \rightarrow \text{ } ^{A}_{Z-1}\text{Y} + \text{ } ^{0}_{+1}\text{e} + \nu$

### Comparison at a Glance

|**Feature**|**Beta-minus (β−)**|**Beta-plus (β+)**|
|---|---|---|
|**Cause**|Excess neutrons|Excess protons|
|**Conversion**|$n^0 \rightarrow p^+$|$p^+ \rightarrow n^0$|
|**Particle Emitted**|Electron ($_{-1}^{0}e$)|Positron ($_{+1}^{0}e$)|
|**Atomic No. ($Z$)**|$+1$|$-1$|
