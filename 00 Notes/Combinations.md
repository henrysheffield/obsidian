# Combinations

## Notes

Order doesn’t matter

Method to arrange things

For example, a combination can calculate how many possible ways you can pick 3 people in a committee from a group of 12. In this case, the order or who was picked first does not matter.

$$ C(n,r)=\binom{n}{r}=\frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!} $$
where
$n$ = total number of items in a set
$r$ = number of items to be chosen

## Example

> [!QUESTION] Fruit Smoothie Combination
> You are at a fruit stand that has 5 different types of fruit: apples, bananas, cherries, dates, and elderberries. You want to buy exactly 3 types of fruit to make a smoothie. How many different combinations of fruit can you choose?
> 
> > [!SUCCESS]- Answer
> > The answer is **10**.
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $$C(5, 3) = \frac{5 \times 4 \times 3}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = 10$$
