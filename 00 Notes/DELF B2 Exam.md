# DELF B2 Junior Preparation Plan

## Strategy Overview
With exactly four weeks until May 29th, the focus is on exam technique and consistent exposure. The B2 Junior exam expects you to defend an opinion, understand news broadcasts, and write formal essays. 

**Daily Minimums:**
- [ ] 15 minutes of passive listening (e.g., *InnerFrench* or *RFI Journal en français facile* while commuting or heading to the gym).
- [ ] 10 minutes of vocabulary review (Anki/Spaced Repetition).

---

## Week 1: Format Familiarization & Baseline (May 2 - May 8)
*Goal: Understand the strict timing and structure of the B2 exam.*

- [ ] Print or download an official DELF B2 Junior past paper.
- [ ] Complete the Reading section under timed conditions (1 hour). 
- [ ] Review mistakes from the Reading section and extract unknown vocabulary into your flashcards.
- [ ] Read one article from *Le Monde* or *Le Figaro* focusing on youth, education, or environment (common B2 Junior themes).
- [ ] Outline a classic *plan dialectique* (intro, arguments for, arguments against, conclusion) for a sample essay prompt.
- [ ] Do a 20-minute shadow-reading session with a news broadcast to warm up your pronunciation.

## Week 2: Input Intensive - Listening & Reading (May 9 - May 15)
*Goal: Train your ear for B2-speed audio and improve reading comprehension.*

- [ ] Complete a timed Listening practice test. Note: the long audio is played twice, the short audio only once.
- [ ] Read a persuasive opinion piece online and highlight the logical connectors (*cependant*, *néanmoins*, *en outre*).
- [ ] Write a 250-word letter of complaint or a letter to the mayor (classic B2 writing task). 
- [ ] Use a timer to practice skimming a long text for the main idea within 3 minutes.
- [ ] Record yourself speaking for 5 minutes on a random societal topic without stopping. Don't worry about mistakes, just maintain flow.

## Week 3: Output Intensive - Writing & Oral (May 16 - May 22)
*Goal: Lock down your transition words and oral defense strategies.*

- [ ] Write a full essay (250 words) under a strict 45-minute time limit.
- [ ] Review your essay specifically checking for subjunctive triggers (e.g., *il faut que*, *bien que*).
- [ ] Prepare an oral presentation outline. You get 30 minutes to prepare for the oral exam; practice creating your bullet points within 20 minutes.
- [ ] Do a mock oral presentation (5-7 minutes monologue) focusing on introducing the topic and stating your stance clearly.
- [ ] Practice defending your viewpoint. Anticipate counter-arguments the examiner might ask and practice responding to them aloud.

## Week 4: Simulation & Polish (May 23 - May 28)
*Goal: Build stamina for the real exam and review.*

- [ ] Take a full, timed mock exam (Reading, Listening, Writing) in one sitting.
- [ ] Grade the mock exam ruthlessly.
- [ ] Review all saved flashcards and vocabulary lists.
- [ ] Do one final mock oral presentation.
- [ ] Prepare your exam day bag: ID, convocation from the Alliance Française, pens, and water.
- [ ] Rest. No heavy studying on May 28th. 

## Exam Day: May 29
- [ ] Arrive at the Alliance Française with plenty of time to spare.
- [ ] Breathe and execute.
