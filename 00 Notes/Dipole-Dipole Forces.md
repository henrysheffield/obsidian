## Dipole-Dipole Forces

**Dipole-dipole forces** are a specific type of intermolecular attraction that occurs between **polar molecules**. These forces manifest when the partial negative charge of one molecule is electrostatically attracted to the partial positive charge of a neighboring molecule.

### Key Concepts

- **Intermolecular:** These are forces _between_ separate molecules, not the bonds _within_ a molecule.
    
- **Polarity Requirement:** These forces only exist in polar molecules (molecules with a permanent dipole).
    

### Case Study: Carbon Monoxide ($CO$)

- **Geometry:** Linear.
    
- **Electronegativity:** The difference in electronegativity ($\Delta EN$) is approximately **1.0**.
    
- **Interaction:** In a sample of $CO$, the Oxygen atom (carrying a partial negative charge, $\delta-$) is attracted to the Carbon atom (carrying a partial positive charge, $\delta+$) of the adjacent molecule.

### Simplified Explanation (Toddler Level)

Imagine you are holding your Mom’s hand. You are holding on very tight because she is super strong—that is like the bond holding one molecule together.

Now, imagine another pair of people (another Mom and kid) walks by. You might reach out with your free hand to give a quick high-five or a light tug on their sleeve. You aren't stuck to them as tightly as you are to your own Mom, but there is still a little "pull" holding you toward them. That weaker, outside pull is a **dipole-dipole attraction**.
