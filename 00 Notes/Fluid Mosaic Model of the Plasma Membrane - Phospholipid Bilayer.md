## Fluid Mosaic Model: The Plasma Membrane

The **Fluid Mosaic Model** describes the cell membrane as a tapestry of several types of molecules (phospholipids, cholesterols, and proteins) that are constantly moving. This movement helps the cell membrane maintain its role as a barrier between the inside and outside of the cell environments.

---

### Key Membrane Components

| **Component**                   | **Role in Membrane**  | **Key Details**                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Phospholipid Bilayer**        | Structural Foundation | Creates a semi-permeable barrier. Heads are **hydrophilic** (water-loving) and tails are **hydrophobic** (water-fearing)                                    |
| **Cholesterol**                 | Fluidity Buffer       | Prevents phospholipids from packing too tight in the cold (acting as spacers) and keeps them together in the heat [                                         |
| **Integral Proteins**           | Transport             | These proteins go **through** the membrane. They are vital for transporting large or polar molecules, like **glucose**, that cannot cross the bilayer alone |
| **Peripheral Proteins**         | Support & Enzymes     | Found on the exterior or interior surfaces. They often act as enzymes or attach to the cytoskeleton to help maintain cell shape                             |
| **Glycoproteins & Glycolipids** | Identification        | Carbohydrates attached to proteins or lipids. They serve as "ID tags" for cell-to-cell recognition (Self vs. Non-self) and signaling                        |
