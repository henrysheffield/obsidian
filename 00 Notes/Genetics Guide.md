# Genetics Guide

## Notes

To get from "next to nothing" to an expert level in genetics quickly, you need a high-efficiency framework that leverages your visual learning style and your specific tech stack (**Obsidian, Anki, YouTube, NotebookLM**).

Because you work at a DNA company, building a rock-solid mental model of physical structures first will make the data processing, sequencing, and informatics parts of your job make sense incredibly fast.

Here is your accelerated, visual-first framework and tool-stack strategy.

## Part 1: The Accelerated Genetics Framework

Move through these four tiers sequentially. Do not move to the next tier until you can mentally "visualise" the processes in the current one.

### Tier 1: The Molecular Foundations (The "Physical" Reality)

Before you look at data, you must see the machinery.

- **The Anatomy of DNA & RNA:** Double helix structure, nucleotide base-pairing ($A-T$, $C-G$), and 5' to 3' directionality.
    
- **The Central Dogma:** Exactly how a physical strand of DNA is copied into RNA (**Transcription**) and read by a ribosome to build a protein (**Translation**).
    
- **Genome Architecture:** How 2 metres of DNA packs into a microscopic cell using histones and chromatin, organizing into 23 pairs of chromosomes.
    

### Tier 2: Transmission & Classical Genetics (How Traits Move)

This is the math and logic of how DNA behaves across generations.

- **Mendelian Inheritance:** Dominant vs. recessive alleles, homozygous vs. heterozygous genotypes, and Punnett squares.
    
- **Cellular Division:** Mitosis (cell cloning) vs. Meiosis (making sperm/egg cells with half the DNA). You must understand **crossing over** in meiosis—this is why siblings aren't identical.
    
- **Non-Mendelian Genetics:** Co-dominance, incomplete dominance, and sex-linked traits (e.g., why colour-blindness is more common in males).
    

### Tier 3: Genomics & Advanced Molecular Genetics (The "Expert" Transition)

This bridges basic science with the work done at modern DNA companies.

- **Gene Regulation:** How cells turn genes "on" or "off" (promoters, enhancers, and epigenetics like DNA methylation).
    
- **Mutations & Variations:** The difference between a Single Nucleotide Polymorphism (**SNP**—the core of consumer DNA testing), insertions, deletions (Indels), and Copy Number Variations (CNVs).
    
- **Mitochondrial & Extra-nuclear DNA:** DNA that doesn't live in the nucleus and is inherited strictly from the mother.
    

### Tier 4: Modern Biotech & Sequencing Technology

Where the science meets industry applications.

- **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction):** The molecular photocopy machine used to amplify DNA samples.
    
- **Sanger Sequencing vs. Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS):** Reading DNA letter-by-letter. Sanger is slow and accurate for single genes; NGS runs millions of fragments simultaneously (high-throughput).
    
- **CRISPR-Cas9:** The programmable molecular scissors used for gene editing.
    

## Part 2: The Integrated Tech Stack Strategy

To learn fast, your tools must work together in a continuous pipeline: **Consume (YouTube) $\rightarrow$ Synthesise (NotebookLM) $\rightarrow$ Organise (Obsidian) $\rightarrow$ Retain (Anki).**

```
 [YouTube / Papers] ➔ [NotebookLM (Synthesise)] ➔ [Obsidian (Map)] ➔ [Anki (Memorise)]
```

### 1. YouTube: Visual Conceptualisation

Because you are a visual learner, **always start here**. Do not read a dry textbook chapter until you have seen an animation of the process.

- **Channels to target:** * _Amoeba Sisters_ (Excellent, highly visual breakdowns of Tier 1 & 2 basics).
    
    - _Crash Course Biology_ (Fast-paced overview of genetic inheritance).
        
    - _Bozeman Science_ or _Khan Academy_ (For deep-diving into specific mechanisms like transcription factors or pedigree charts).
        
- **Action:** Watch at $1.25\times$ or $1.5\times$ speed just to get the spatial/visual map of what is happening. Screenshot crucial diagrams.
    

### 2. NotebookLM: Your AI Study Partner & Explainer

NotebookLM shines at synthesising complex source materials into clear, structured summaries.

- **Action:** Find high-quality, open-source genetics PDFs (like chapters from _OpenStax Biology_ or review papers on NGS sequencing) and upload them as sources.
    
- **The Prompt Strategy:** Ask NotebookLM to explain concepts to you using analogies or visual descriptions.
    
    > _"Explain the difference between Sanger Sequencing and Next-Generation Sequencing using a printing press analogy. Generate a comparative summary table."_
    
- You can also use its **Audio Overview** feature to generate a podcast-style conversation about your uploaded genetics documents to listen to while commuting.
    

### 3. Obsidian: Building Your "Genome" of Knowledge

Use Obsidian to map how concepts connect. Genetics is highly interconnected; a mutation in Tier 3 links directly to a phenotype in Tier 2.

- **Atomic Notes:** Create a single note for individual concepts (e.g., a note titled `[[Exon]]`, a note titled `[[Intron]]`, and a note titled `[[RNA Splicing]]`).
    
- **Visual Links:** Embed your YouTube screenshots directly into your notes. Use Obsidian's **Canvas** feature to visually map out paths, such as drawing arrows from `[[DNA]]` $\rightarrow$ `[[mRNA]]` $\rightarrow$ `[[Protein]]`.
    

### 4. Anki: Locking It In Permanently

Genetics has a massive vocabulary. If you don't know the words, you can't understand the data. Use Anki daily to ensure zero knowledge decay.

- **Image Occlusion Enhanced:** This is a vital plugin for visual learners. Instead of writing text cards, take a diagram of a DNA replication fork or a meiotic stage, hide the labels, and test yourself on them.
    
- **Conceptual Cloze Deletions:** Keep your text cards concise and focused on relationships:
    
    > "Transcription occurs in the {{c1::nucleus}}, while translation occurs in the {{c2::cytoplasm/ribosome}}."
