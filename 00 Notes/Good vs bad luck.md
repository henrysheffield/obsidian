# Good vs bad luck

>”It is my bad luck that this has happened to me. No, you should rather say: ‘It is my good luck that, although this has happened to me, I can bear it without pain, neither crushed by the present nor fearful of the future … So why see more misfortune in the event than good fortune in your ability to bear it?”
>4.49

Powerful notion of completely changing the concept of ‘bad luck’ from a grave annoyance to admiring that you were able to bear the pain.

**Example**

Stubbing your toe.

Yes, it can hurt to stub your toe–for about 30 seconds–but then it feels better. Rather then letting this ruin your day or swearing at your annoyance, you should be proud that you could bear the pain without crying, without tearing up, without rolling on the floor sobbing. It is useless to be angry of this past event because it is [[Dichotomy#Philosophy|out of our control]], instead concentrate on your [[Good vs bad luck|good fortune to bear the pain.]]
