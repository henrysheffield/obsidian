## Gram Positive Bacteria

### Summary

> [!ABSTRACT] Description
> Gram Positive bacteria are a type of bacteria with a much thicker cell wall made of [[Peptioglycan]] compared to [[Gram Negative Bacteria]]
> 

### Key Ideas

- **Cell Wall:** Bacteria have a cell wall. Gram Positive bacteria generally have much thicker cell walls of $20-80~nm$ compared to Gram Negative. This also means that there is more Peptioglycan in the cell wall.
    
- **[[Gram Staining]]:** Fundamental technique in microbiology to distinguish between the two bacteria by their colour. Gram Positive appears purple, Gram Negative appears red/pink.
	→ The Gram Positive bacteria retains the violet stain due to its thicker cell wall, making it appear violet/purple.
    

---

### Properties

- **Cell Wall Structure:** They possess a thick, multilayered [[Peptioglycan]] layer that makes up about 50% to 90% of the cell wall.
    
- **Outer Membrane:** They **lack** an outer lipid membrane.
    
- **Gram Stain Result:** They retain the crystal violet dye during the staining process and appear **purple** or **blue** under a microscope.
    
- **Examples:** Common examples include: [[Staphylococcus Aureus]] and _Streptococcus pyogenes_.
