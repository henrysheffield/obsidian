# How to ACE your VCE English Exam (from 99.95 ATAR student)

![How to ACE your VCE English Exam (from 99.95 ATAR student)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CL8CcMkx6Bw)

## Notes

![[How to ACE your VCE English Exam (from 99.95 ATAR student)-1785538171146.webp]]*Planning image example (doesn’t have to be in full sentences)*

### Planning

- Create detailed plans–not endless essays
- Outside of the essay, just look at prompts and create plans (10 - 15 minutes each)
- These plans are detailed with: *contention, body pargraphs (info + dotpoints)*
- Memorise plans with anki
- Created about 100 plans in anki (10 - 15 minutes each = 25 hours of time - can achieve this by 1 hour per day for 25 days !!!)
- Twice per week, he would write a full essay based on a certain plan

### Plan Structure:

> Contention
> 3 Topic Sentences
> 3 - 4 quotes per paragraph
> Brief analysis ideas

### Writing the essay

> [!TIP] 
> Before writing any essay, write 3 things at the top of your essay which you need to focus on
> Example: 
> 1. No personal pronouns (us, we)
>  2. 15 minutes per body paragraph 
>  3. Analyse the why of x object

- Wrote 1-2 essays per week 
- Wrote ~2 argument analysis per week 
- Used past [[VCAA]] prompts for essays + argument analysis

> [!TIP]
> Examiners often judge in intro and first paragraph, make sure these two are in your neatest handwriting possible

### Time management

- Each body paragraph is 250 - 300 words
- Write an essay and examine how large you writing is, this can help you keep track of how much you need to write
- Conclusion sometimes skipped, body paragraphs more important

#### Structure

| Time | Focus                         |
| ---- | ----------------------------- |
| 15   | Intro + Planning + Conclusion |
| 15   | Paragraph 1                   |
| 15   | Paragraph 2                   |
| 15   | Paragraph 3                   |


### Article Analysis?

1. Read article to get the basic idea
2. Choose quotes / evidence (mark these with numbers)
3. This should take 2 - 3 mins

> [!TIP]
> Answer the prompt!
> Use 1. Explicit stating of the prompt 2. Synonyms / Similar words to the prompt


- Always challenge the prompt
- Proof read last
