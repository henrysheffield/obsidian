# How to Journal (Like Marcus Aurelius)

![How to Journal (Like Marcus Aurelius)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YOgBiweyEE4)

## Notes

- Book 1 = A gratitude journal, Marcus demonstrates who he is and who/what he appreciates
- Before knowing where you want to go (e.g. a future job / course) you need to understand *who you are and where you come from*
- Marcus was writing to himself, not for others → your journal is a private piece, be honest with yourself and don’t say things in a euphemistic manner

**Inner Citadel** - The inner privacy of your mind 
→ In Marcus’ case, it was his books

- Marcus was frequently disturbed by others, throughout his journal he expresses clear discontent, but also corrects himself to not bother with judgements of others
	→ This is just the [[Dichotomy of Control]]

**Dyeing the mind** - Following [[The Four Virtues of Stoicism|the Stoic Principles]]
→ Marcus applies to every opportunity, and repeats it
→ Writes the situation and result in his journals–you need to do this too

**Example:** 

1. *Showed courage today, went to a job interview and attempted to be relaxed and not nervous–I believe it worked but I have no control over the result.

2. *Showed courage today, spoke Infront of a big audience on an issue, realised that I can’t control the audience’s emotions, but I can strongly influence them.*

The above examples demonstrates me repeating myself (practice makes perfect), Stoic principles (Dichotomy of control) and progress
- Journaling makes you discover yourself

## Application

- [ ] Do more gratitude journaling
- [ ] Finish *Meditations* on another day
- [ ] Follow Stoic Principles every single day and write the result + situations in your journal. 🔺 

### Summary

- Write down what your grateful for
- Write down when your opportunities for Stoic virtues today and how you reacted to them
- Writing is the **inner citadel**–a place where you can be you
	- Be honest with yourself
	- Don’t say anything in a euphemistic manner
	- It is just you and your thoughts
