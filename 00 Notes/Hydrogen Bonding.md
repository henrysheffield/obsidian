# Hydrogen Bonding

## Notes

Strongest **intermolecular force** which occurs when a hydrogen atom boned to $N, O ~or~F$ is attracted to a lone pair of electrons in another molecule.

The hydrogen atom must be bonded to Nitrogen, Oxygen or Fluorine (remember by *NOF*)

The donor = molecule with the H attached to N, O or F
The acceptor = A lone pair of electrons on N, O or F of a *neighbouring molecule*

## Example 2: Water → Steam

$$H_{2}O_{(l)} \rightarrow H_{2} O_{(g)}$$
- In liquid water, the water molecules are close to each other. This is due to hydrogen bonds.
- Via an endothermic reaction (heat is added to liquid water) the hydrogen bonds break down, causing the molecules to have a greater distance.
- Causes the liquid state to transition to a gaseous state
