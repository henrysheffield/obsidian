# Bulk-Batch Korean Ground Turkey

This recipe reimagines a classic Korean beef bowl using lean ground turkey. It relies on a sweet, savory, and umami-rich soy-sesame sauce that coats the meat perfectly, making it an excellent high-protein meal prep option that stays moist when reheated.

- **Total Time:** 20 minutes
    
- **Yield:** 4 to 5 portions
    
- **Cookware:** 1 large deep skillet or wok
    

---

## Ingredients

### The Meat and Aromatics

- 1 kg Turkey / Beef mince
    
- 1 tablespoon Sesame oil (for cooking)
    
- 4 cloves Garlic, finely minced
    
- 1 tablespoon Fresh ginger, finely grated
    
- 1 bunch Green onions (scallions), finely sliced (separate the white bottoms from the green tops)
    

### The Korean Glaze Sauce

- 1/2 cup Soy sauce
    
- 1/4 cup Water
    
- 3 tablespoons Brown sugar (or honey)
    
- 1 tablespoon Toasted sesame oil
    
- 1 tablespoon Sriracha or gochujang (optional, adjust for desired heat)
    
- 1/2 teaspoon Black pepper
    

---

## Method

### 1. Whisk the Sauce

In a small bowl, combine the soy sauce, water, brown sugar, one tablespoon of toasted sesame oil, black pepper, and sriracha or gochujang if you want a subtle kick. Whisk the mixture thoroughly until the sugar completely dissolves. Set the bowl aside.

### 2. Sauté the Aromatics and Brown the Turkey

Place your large skillet or wok over medium-high heat and add the cooking sesame oil. Toss in the minced garlic, grated ginger, and the white sliced bottoms of the green onions. Sauté for 1 to 2 minutes. Immediately add the 1 kg of turkey mince to the pan. Use a firm spatula to break the meat apart continuously as it cooks for 6 to 8 minutes.

> **Visual Cue:** The turkey will release some moisture and turn from pink to an **opaque, matte white and light grey crumbly texture**. Ensure there are no pink clusters left before moving to the next step.

### 3. Glaze and Reduce

Pour the prepared sauce mixture directly over the browned turkey. Stir well to ensure the meat is completely submerged. Lower the heat to medium and let the mixture simmer gently for 5 minutes, stirring occasionally.

> **Visual Cue:** At first, the sauce will look thin and watery at the bottom of the pan. You are finished cooking when the liquid reduces and thickens into a **dark, glossy brown glaze** that clings to the meat. When you push the turkey aside with your spoon, the sauce should not pool instantly back into the empty space.

### 4. Garnish and Rest

Turn off the heat. Toss the remaining green sliced tops of the green onions directly into the pan and stir them through the warm meat.

> **Visual Cue:** The residual heat will soften the green onions slightly, making them look **bright, vibrant green** against the dark, glazed turkey.

---

## Meal Prep Tips

- **Controlling the Sweetness:** Lean turkey mince benefits greatly from sugar because it helps create a sticky glaze that mimics a traditional bulgogi texture. If you are watching your sugar intake, you can swap the brown sugar for a granulated low-calorie sweetener, though it will yield a slightly less sticky finish.
    
- **Serving Layout:** This pairs perfectly with steamed basmati or jasmine rice and a side of quick-pickled cucumbers or steamed broccoli. To keep it visually organized in your meal prep containers, place a bed of rice on one side and a generous scoop of the glazed turkey on the other, allowing some of the extra sauce to soak into the rice.
    
- **Easy Reheating:** Because turkey is ultra-lean, add a tiny splash of water or low-sodium chicken broth into your meal prep container just before microwaving. This creates a small amount of steam under the lid, keeping the meat perfectly tender and preventing it from drying out.
