## The Kármán Line: Boundary of Space

The **Kármán Line** is the most widely accepted boundary separating the Earth's atmosphere from outer space. Situated within the **Thermosphere**, it serves as a critical transition point for both physics and international law.

---

### Key Technical Aspects

- **Altitude:** Located exactly **100 km** (approx. 62 miles) above mean sea level.
    
- **Aeronautical Significance:** At this altitude, the atmosphere becomes too thin to support conventional aerodynamic flight. For a vehicle to generate enough lift to remain airborne, it would have to travel faster than **orbital velocity**.
    
- **Regulatory Body:** Formally recognized by the **Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI)**, an international record-keeping body for aeronautics and astronautics.

- **Atmospheric Appearance:** From this height, the Earth’s atmosphere is visible only as a thin, glowing blue sliver against the horizon.
    
- **Sky Color:** The scattering of sunlight (Rayleigh scattering) ceases to be effective due to the low air density, causing the sky to appear pitch black even during daylight hours.
