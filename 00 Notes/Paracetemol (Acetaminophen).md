## Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)

**Paracetamol** is a common **non-opioid analgesic** and **antipyretic**. While it effectively treats mild-to-moderate pain and reduces fever, it is distinct from NSAIDs like Diclofenac because it lacks significant anti-inflammatory properties and does not cause the same level of gastric irritation.

### Structure

![[Paracetemol (Acetaminophen)-1776577196075.webp]]

- **Molecular Formula:** $C_8H_9NO_2$
    
- **Functional Groups:** 
	- **Benzene Ring:** A stable six-carbon hexagonal core.
    
    - **Hydroxyl Group ($-OH$):** Specifically a phenolic hydroxyl group attached to the ring.
        
    - **Secondary Amide Group:** An nitrogen-containing group ($-NHCOCH_3$) attached to the opposite side of the ring.
        

### Physical and Chemical Properties

- **Appearance:** A white, odorless, crystalline solid at room temperature.
    
- **Molecular Weight:** Approximately **151.16 g/mol**.
    
- **Melting Point:** **169°C - 171°C**.
    
- **Intermolecular Forces:** Capable of forming strong **hydrogen bonds**, primarily due to the hydroxyl group, which influences its crystal structure and boiling point.
    
- **Solubility:** * **Water:** Sparingly soluble in cold water; solubility increases significantly in hot water.
    
    - **Lipids:** Moderately lipid-soluble, allowing it to pass through cell membranes and the **blood-brain barrier** with ease.
        
- **Acidity:** It is a weakly acidic compound.
    

---

### Comparison: Paracetamol vs. NSAIDs

Unlike NSAIDs (like Diclofenac), Paracetamol does not significantly inhibit peripheral prostaglandin synthesis. This makes it gentler on the stomach lining but less effective for reducing swelling or redness.
