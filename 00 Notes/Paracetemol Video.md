### Quick Summary

Paracetamol is a widely used analgesic and antipyretic that evolved from the drug phenacetin, primarily targeting the central nervous system to reduce pain and fever.

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VHgcEvckjtM&t=1s)


### Names

- **Chemical/Scientific Name:** Acetaminophen
    
- **USA Brand Name:** Tylenol
    
- **International Brand Name:** Paracetamol
    

### Mechanism of Action

While often grouped with **NSAIDs** (Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs), paracetamol is distinct:

- **COX Inhibition:** It weakly inhibits [[COX enzymes]]
    
- **Primary Function:** Highly effective for **pain (analgesia)** and **fever (antipyresis)**.
    
- **The Inflammation Gap:** Unlike Ibuprofen or Diclofenac, it lacks significant anti-inflammatory properties.
    

### Clinical Distinctions: CNS vs. Peripheral

- **Paracetamol:** Primarily acts on the **Central Nervous System (CNS)**. This localized action in the brain explains why it manages systemic pain/fever but ignores localized swelling.
    
- **Traditional NSAIDs:** These act **peripherally**. By inhibiting COX enzymes at the site of injury (e.g., a swollen joint), they effectively reduce physical inflammation.
