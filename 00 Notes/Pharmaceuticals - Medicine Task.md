# Pharmaceuticals - Medicine Task

Research one bush medicine plant that interests you. (First Nation Australians related) You will share your reasearch by yarning with another student.

## Questions

1. What is the plant traditionally used for?
2. Which part of the plant is used?
3. How is it prepared or applied?
4. What active compounds may be present? 
5. Is there scientific evidence supporting its effectiveness?
6. What ethical considerations apply when studying this plant?

## Plant

The Witchetty Bush (_Acacia kempeana_)

![[Pharmaceuticals - Medicine Task-1780359225903.webp]]

**1. What is it used for?**
- Respiratory relief (e.g. treatment for coughs)
- Wound healing: used as an antimicrobial wash to treat skin infections
- **Nutrition:** High in protein and very healthy

**2. What part of the plant is used?**
- Stems
- Bark
- Leaves / Twigs (for respiratory issues and general washes)

**3. How is it prepared?**
- Can be inhaled (plants are placed directly on hot coals and patient inhales the gas)
- Can be boiled (crush the leaves to create a strong medicated liquid wash for infected skin)
- Edible paste: Gums and resins extracted from the trunk can be mixed to create a smooth, edible paste.

**4. What active compounds may be present?**
- **Tannins:** Highly concentrated in the bark, tannins act as an astringents, they bind to tissues, which stops bleeding, tightens skin, and protects open wounds.
    
- **Flavonoids:** Powerful antioxidants that exhibit strong anti-inflammatory properties, reducing swelling and pain in the body.
    
- **Saponins:** Soap-like chemical compounds that disrupt microbial cell membranes, functioning as a natural antiseptic/antibacterial agent.

**5. Is there scientific evidence supporting its effectiveness?**

While the _Acacia_ genus shows general antimicrobial and antioxidant potential, specific scientific research validating the traditional medicinal efficacy of _Acacia kempeana_ is very limited.

**6. What ethical considerations apply when studying this plant?**

Research requires strict adherence to Indigenous cultural protocol, ensuring informed consent and equitable benefit-sharing with Aboriginal communities to protect their traditional knowledge.
