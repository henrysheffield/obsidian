## Summary

Unstable (radioactive) isotope.
## Description

A radioisotope is an unstable isotope due to the ratio of neutrons and protons. This causes the nucleus to become unstable.

> [!TIP]
> To reach a stable state, the nucleus undergoes [[Radioactive Decay]]
> Emits radiation in the process

## Example

Carbon-14 is an unstable isotope of Carbon, it has a half life of approximately 5,730 years. It is used in carbon dating. For instance, if an organism died with 10 g of Carbon-14, in 5,730 years it would have about 5 g left.

> [!WARNING] 
> There is no difference between the term *radionuclide* and *radioisotope* they are basically the same thing.
> 
> However *radionuclide* can be a more ‘general’ term for a radioactive atom.

### Flashcards

Define a radioisotope? :: A radioisotope (or radionuclide) is ==an unstable version of a chemical element that releases radiation—such as alpha, beta, or gamma rays—as it decays to become more stable==. These atoms have an imbalanced number of neutrons and protons, causing them to emit energy. They are used extensively in medicine for imaging and cancer therapy, in industry for tracking, and in research.
