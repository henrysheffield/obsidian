# Reference sheet - stoicism

## Notes

Stoicism is a practical philosophy designed to help you build resilience, clarity, and peace of mind. As a visual learner, think of Stoicism as a framework for **filtering** your experiences into two distinct categories, allowing you to focus your energy only on what you can truly control.

Here are the core principles you need to know:

### 1. The Dichotomy of Control

This is the cornerstone of Stoic practice. You must constantly distinguish between what is up to you and what is not.

- **Within your control:** Your own thoughts, actions, intentions, and character. These are the only things that can truly cause you "harm" or "good," because they define your integrity.
    
- **Outside your control:** The weather, traffic, other people's opinions, past events, and external outcomes.
    

### 2. Living in Accordance with Nature (Reason)

For Stoics, "Nature" refers to both the physical universe and human nature. Humans are social, rational beings. To live well, you must use your **reason** to navigate the world rather than being driven by impulsive emotions. When you face a difficult situation, ask: _Is this in line with my rational values, or am I reacting out of fear or frustration?_

### 3. Perception is Everything

Stoicism teaches that it is not events themselves that disturb us, but our **judgement** about those events.

- An external event (like failing a test) is neutral.
    
- Your interpretation ("I am a failure") is what causes the suffering.
    
    If you change your perception, you change your reality.
    

### 4. Amor Fati (Love of Fate)

Rather than just tolerating what happens to you, the Stoics suggest you should **embrace it**. Everything that happens is part of the interconnected web of the universe. By welcoming whatever occurs—whether good or bad—you remain unshakeable. You turn every obstacle into an opportunity to practice virtue.

### 5. The Four Cardinal Virtues

Stoics believe that character is the only "good." To build a strong character, you should aim to act with these four virtues in every situation:

- **Wisdom:** Understanding the world clearly and acting logically.
    
- **Justice:** Treating others with fairness and kindness.
    
- **Courage:** Facing fears and challenges with integrity.
    
- **Temperance:** Practicing self-control and moderation in all things.
    

### How to apply this as a student

You can track these principles in your Obsidian setup. For example, you could create a **"Stoic Reflection"** template in your daily log:

1. **Morning:** What challenges might I face today, and how will I apply the dichotomy of control?
    
2. **Evening:** Where did I let external events dictate my mood? Did I act with virtue?
