# "Remove the judgement, and you have removed the thought ‘I am hurt’ remove the thought ‘I am hurt’, and the hurt itself is removed."



Marcus Aurelius’ *Meditations* demonstrates how the vast majority of pain is exemplified by the brain, and how it can be softened by merely changing one’s thought process. Aurelius had to deal with an ubiquitous amount of pain, twelve dead children, a plague in Rome and experiences in war. Nevertheless, Aurelius taught himself how to bear the pain, primarily by being disciplined and understanding where the ache comes from. 

According to him, we can simply “Remove the judgement", and you have removed the though ‘I am hurt’” removing the “judgement” means that we must halt increasing the pain, never say ‘ouch’ or focus on the pain–this naturally lets you imagine other things. The author also adds, “remove the thought ‘I am hurt’ and the hurt itself is removed” creating a remarkable way to manage pain–one can simply stop the ache and continue with their day.

Aurelius’ strategy of easing pain is simple, anytime that an unpleasant feeling occurs to a human, one can just remove the thought “I am hurt”, illustrating that pain is just in the mind. A core stoic principle is to not avoid pain–but instead embrace it–allowing us to understand how to cope with it. For example, we can feel indifferent to pain by taking cold showers–an unpleasant at first feeling but satisfactory experience–or try your absolute hardest in a muay thai session. Yes, the fatigue may be great, but you will almost always feel better afterwards.

In conclusion, Aurelius advises to himself to cope with the pain by merely removing his inner judgement of himself. He also simply removes the thought process of aching fatigue. To apply these principles in real life, we can embrace pain during cold showers or training hard during your few muay thai sessions.
