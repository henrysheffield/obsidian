# Titration

## Notes

Titration is a method to find an unknown concentration of a solution by slowly adding a second solution of a **known** concentration until a reaction occurs.

## Steps

### 1. Clean Burette and Pipette
- Essential to prevent altering of concentration
- Careful with pipette, it is hard to clean
- You might accidentally ‘crucifix’ the pipette

### 2. Add acid of a known volume but **an unknown concentration** to a flask

> [!TIP]
> Label the two beakers containing the acid and the base as they are both identical colourless liquids

### 3. Add indicator

- Add 3 drops of phenalphthalein [^1]
- Used to see the reaction (e.g. solution will turn into a pinkish-purplish colour

### 4. Initial reading of volume of titrant taken

- Measure from the burette
- Measure from an arbitrary point that correlates with your height (so it is not too difficult to record measurements)

### 5. Titrating

- Titrant slowly released into acid solution of the flask
- Flask continuely swirled to ensure even mixing

### 6. End

- When the solution changes colour (reaction has occured)
- Stop the titrant from flowing
- Record final volume of the burette

$$\text{Final Reading} - \text{Initial Reading} = \text{Titer Volume}$$
(From the video: $19.6\text{ mL} - 13.8\text{ mL} = 5.8\text{ mL}$ of base used).
