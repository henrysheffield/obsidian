# Treasure every hour

>  “Every hour of the day give vigorous attention … to the performance of the task in hand with precise analysis, with unaffected dignity, with human sympathy, with dispassionate justice … as it were the last of your life.”

Plan:
- Every task, todo is meaningful
- Complete your todo list like it is the last of your life
- Make achieveable goals that will be amazing–unaffected dignity
- Link to mortality theme, it is urgent

Essay:

In Marcus Aurelius’ *Meditations*, the notion of treasuring every day’s minutes is exemplified. The collection of books describes how it is imperative to not waste precious time that could be used to complete tasks and achieve goals. Throughout the extracts, this emotion is constantly related to the everlasting reality of [[Mortality|mortality]]. If we are to die soon, then we must complete all of our goals crazily.

Throughout *Meditations*, Marcus hopes to improve himself by operating every task at hand with fastidious dedication. Many of his to-do’s were not simple, they could be battling a tribe to managing a nation. Aurelius treats these tasks as they are, extremely urgent that must be completed before his inevitable death.

Aurelius writes in his notebooks, “Every hour of the day give vigorous attention,” emphasising the critical importance of the time within a day. He then adds, “with precise analysis,” displaying the level of dedication to each task, making it a mandatory act to always do. Moreover, the lingering feeling of mortality is omnipresent, “as it were the last of your life” Marcus adds, to reinforce the imperative of how to treat a task.

Using Marcus’ annotations, we can improve ourselves at achieving tasks and goals, by giving every hour of the day “vigorous attention” we can appreciate the limited time given in a day, in order to maximise the potential. For instance, the morning hours for most are strictly limited to about 4 hours, if we do not complete tasks in this time frame with “precise analysis,” then we might of wasted it completely. Furthermore, Marcus addresses the reality of death, demanding that the jobs at hand need to be treated “like the last of your life.”

In conclusion, Aurelius’ journal entries create a reality of the short period of one day, and highlights that tasks are more than important. They are objects that should be treated as the last things to do in a life. The urgency that is created in his writing, can improve on how we view tasks. By using this urgency philosophy, we can carry out jobs with higher effort, be more passionate and achieve more.
