# Your goal is to listen

A key aspect of Stoicism is showing respect to others constantly==;==[^4] we can achieve this by actively listening to others, while limiting our own talk to an absolute minimum. At every opportunity to talk to someone, the conversation must focus on them—not oneself; by doing this we make the other person feel appreciated and respected. Epictetus guides this strategy, emphasising, "Let silence be the goal for the most part" as a primary focus. However, it is not completely true to be "silent" the entire time, we must remain mostly quiet, but often ask engaging questions to keep the conversation flowing. Epictetus agrees, stating w/ this ==contradiction==[^5][^6], "say what is only necessary". The active questions are essential in a conversation. ==In the real world==[^7], we can apply these principles easily. If you meet a new person, never talk to much yourself, instead guide the conversation by concentrating on the other person and actively asking follow up questions like, "What was the highlight of that experience?". This way, we can remain silent, brief with responses and keep the ==conversation moving==[^9]. Ultimately, by using Epictetus' ideas of being quiet but engaged, we can show ==tremendous respect==[^8] to others in ==everyday conversation.==[^10]
