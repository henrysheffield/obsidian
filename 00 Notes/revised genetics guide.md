## Notes

### Tier 1: Foundations

- [x] DNA (Nucleic acid which contains genes, which are instructors for proteins to carry out every function in a living organism – basically dictates how a living thing should live) ✅ 2026-06-24
- [x] RNA (3 main types: mRNA, tRNA and rRNA - usually used for transporting protein, being a messenger for instructions and is involved in translation and transcription) ✅ 2026-06-24
- [x] Double helix structure (DNA is made of two antiparallel long chains of nucleic acids (phosphate group + deoxyribose sugar + nitrogenous base - ACTG) ✅ 2026-06-24
- [x] Base pairing (AT or AU and CG) ✅ 2026-06-24
- [ ] 5’ to 3’ directionality
- [x] Transcription (Process where RNA Polymerase reads a gene sequence and creates a messenger RNA to ‘message’ the instructions for coding a protein) ✅ 2026-06-24
- [x] Translation (process where protein is transported from nucleus to a ribosome to be created) ✅ 2026-06-24
- [x] Chromosomes (small structures that contain a long chain of DNA which tightly wrapped around proteins called histones) ✅ 2026-06-24

### Tier 2: Transmission and classic genetics

- [x] Mendelian Inheritance
	- [x] Dominant vs. recessive alleles (some traits are more dominant e.g. brown eyes > blue eyes) an organism will receive dominant typically in a 3:1 ratio, it is pure chance) ✅ 2026-06-24
	- [x] homozygous vs. heterozygous genotypes (homozygous means same e.g. $BB$ whereas heterozygous = different e.g. $Bb$) ✅ 2026-06-24
	- [x] Punnett squares. (simple method of visualising mendelian inheritance) ✅ 2026-06-24
- [x] Cellular division ✅ 2026-06-24
	- [x] Mitosis (cell cloning) vs. Meiosis (making sperm/egg cells with half the DNA). ✅ 2026-06-24
	- [x] You must understand **crossing over** in meiosis—this is why siblings aren't identical. ✅ 2026-06-24
- [ ] Non Mendelian Genetics
	- [ ] Co-dominance
	- [ ] incomplete dominance
	- [ ] sex-linked traits (e.g., why colour-blindness is more common in males). 
		- [ ] Possibly because of the XY Chromosome, where the Y chromosome is shorter →males can have one allele only, which can be the recessive. Therefore, traits like colour blindness are affected by this one chocie.

### Tier 4: Modern technology

- [x] **PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction):** The molecular photocopy machine used to amplify DNA samples.  
- [ ] **Sanger Sequencing vs. Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS):** Reading DNA letter-by-letter. Sanger is slow and accurate for single genes; NGS runs millions of fragments simultaneously (high-throughput).
- [ ] **CRISPR-Cas9:** The programmable molecular scissors used for gene editing.
